privacy

How your data is handled

Short version: your figures are yours. Nobody sees them unless you switch sharing on for a specific person, and the reporting export is stripped of anything that identifies you.

What is stored

Your email address, a display name you choose, and the bills and income you enter — a description, an amount, a category, and the day of the month. That is the whole of it. No bank connection, no account numbers, no transactions, no card details. Nothing is imported from anywhere; every row exists because someone typed it in.

Who can see it

  • You, always.
  • A person you have connected with — but only after you accept the connection and switch on “Share my figures with them”. Those are two separate steps. Accepting a connection on its own reveals nothing.
  • Sharing is read-only and one-directional. They can look; they cannot edit or delete. Them sharing with you does not share you with them — each side controls its own switch, and either of you can turn it off at any moment.
  • Nobody else. Not other users of the app, and not people you have merely been invited by.

How that is enforced

In the database, not in this web page. Every request runs through Postgres row-level security policies that check who is asking before returning a single row. The code running in your browser is public and could be modified by anyone — so it is never trusted to make that decision. If someone rewrote this page to ask for another person's bills, the database would return nothing.

You can read the exact rules: they are in supabase/schema.sql in the public repository.

The reporting export

The project owner can export data to build charts in Power BI — averages, category breakdowns, how a typical month is distributed. That export is pseudonymised. It contains:

  • An opaque household key — a random identifier with no link back to a name or an email, so rows from the same household can be grouped without identifying whose they are.
  • The amount, the category, and the day of the month.

It deliberately excludes your name, your email, and the free-text description of each bill. That last one matters: a description like “Aspen estate mortgage” names a household on its own, so it never leaves the database.

The export is used for aggregate reporting — averages, distributions and charts. It is not used to look at any individual's finances, and it is not shared or sold to anyone.

Why "pseudonymised" and not "anonymised". The household key is random, but it is the same key for you every time — that is what makes grouping possible. Anyone holding the database could therefore match a key back to an account. Genuinely anonymous data cannot be traced back by anyone, and this can, so it would be wrong to call it that. It is still personal data and it is still treated as such.

Where it lives

The database is a Supabase project (hosted Postgres) in a single region. Passwords are hashed by Supabase's authentication service and are never visible to the app or its owner. This site itself is static files on GitHub Pages and holds no data.

Deleting your data

Delete individual bills and income from the app at any time; removal is immediate.

To delete everything, use Delete my account on the Account page. It removes your account, every bill and income record, and every connection, straight away — no request to anyone and no waiting period. It cannot be undone, so download your data first if you want to keep a copy.

Being straight about the limits

This is a personal project built for a household, not a regulated financial product. It has not been independently security-audited. It is a reasonable place to plan a budget; it is not the place for anything you would be seriously harmed by losing. Keep your own copy of anything that matters — the export button is right there.
